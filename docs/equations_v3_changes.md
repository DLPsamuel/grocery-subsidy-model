# Equation cards v3: what changed from MAIN v2, and why

The cards in [`figures/equations_v3/`](../figures/equations_v3/) show the equations as the model actually runs them, in [`code/model.py`](../code/model.py) and [`code/run_model.py`](../code/run_model.py). Only the 17 equations whose form differs from [MAIN spec v2](archive/MAIN_NYC_Grocery_Subsidy_Model_Specification_v2.md) were re-rendered. Each file keeps the number of its MAIN v2 card in [`figures/equations/`](../figures/equations/), so `eq_02` in both folders is the same equation, old and new.

- **Generated by:** [`code/render_equations_v3.py`](../code/render_equations_v3.py).
- **Values on the cards:** from the base run, `results/chris_run0_calibration/`.
- **Samuel's run:** [`code/samuel_run.py`](../code/samuel_run.py) calls the same functions, so it uses the same equations. Its only difference is that \(\beta_p\) is not scaled to N.Y.C. Groceries' capacity (see Change 4 below).

**Notation.** The cards use the notation of the slide image [`figures/eq_utility_V_ij.png`](../figures/eq_utility_V_ij.png): \(\alpha_j\), \(\gamma_{sq}\), \(sq_j\), \(b_i\) and \(d_{ij}\). MAIN uses \(\beta_{\text{sqft}}\), \(\text{sqft}_j\) and \(d_{tj}\). Distance is written \(-\beta_d d_{ij}\) with \(\beta_d = 0.548 > 0\). The code stores `BETA_D = -0.5481` and adds it, which gives the same number.

## The five changes behind every update

These changes were agreed by the team on 2026-10-02 and 2026-10-03; they're recorded in the docstrings of `code/model.py` and `code/params.py`. Every card below traces back to one or more of them.

1. **Store-type constants \(\alpha\) (`model.calibrate`).**
   - Each store type gets a constant: supermarket −1.71, small independent −3.10.
   - **Purpose:** they absorb everything that size, price and distance don't explain, such as quality, layout and habit.
   - **How they're set:** a Berry (1994) contraction, \(\alpha_t \leftarrow \alpha_t + \ln(R_t / \hat R_t)\), adjusts them until predicted sales for each store type equal the observed v2 revenue \(R_j\) for that type. This is the model's only link to observed sales. It is re-run for every parameter set, because \(\alpha\) absorbs whatever the \(\beta\)s leave unexplained.
2. **Outside option, "other stores", with \(V_{i0} = 0\) (`model.shares`, `model.logsum`).**
   - This option stands for bodegas and stores outside CD2.
   - **Purpose:** the 9 modeled stores capture only about 49% of CD2 grocery spending (observed \(R_j\) total over \(M\)). Without an outside option, the model would force 100% of spending into the 9 stores. With it, a price cut or a new store can draw shoppers away from other stores, not just shuffle them among the 9.
   - Its \(e^{0} = 1\) is the "1 +" that now appears in every denominator and logsum.
3. **Price per trip, \(\beta_{p,i}\, b_i\,(p_j - \bar p)\) (`model.utility`, `model.baskets_per_trip`).**
   - \(b_i = 52\,\bar f_g / (T \bar p)\) is baskets bought per trip: Low 2.83, Mid 3.27, High 5.10.
   - **Purpose:** a household chooses a store once per trip and buys several baskets on that trip, so the price term should reflect the dollars at stake on the whole trip. Without \(b_i\), utility would count only one basket's price difference, however much the household buys: \$82 a week for Low and \$147 for High.
   - **Why \(\bar p\) (\$28.83):** it prices the outside option at the mean basket price. NYC DOHMH Epi Data Brief 158 finds South Bronx bodega and supermarket baskets cost about the same.
4. **\(\beta_p\) scaled to N.Y.C. Groceries' capacity (`model.calibrate_beta_p_scale`).**
   - **The problem:** at the value derived from the value of travel time (USDOT), the store-level own-price elasticity is about 19. At 30% off, N.Y.C. Groceries would then sell about \$56M a year: half of all CD2 spending, and 4.7 times what a 15,000 sq ft store can sell.
   - **The fix:** the level of \(\beta_p\) is scaled so the store sells its capacity, 15,000 sq ft × \$801 = \$12.0M a year. The income gradient is kept.
   - **Resulting values:** Low 0.0583, Mid 0.0195, High 0.0084. The symbol is unchanged, so this changes the values on the cards, not the form of any equation.
5. **Predicted sales instead of observed revenue (`model.sales`).**
   - Predicted sales are \(\hat R_j = \sum_i n_i T s_{ij} b_i p_j\).
   - **Purpose:** volumes and costs move with the model's own demand, so a policy that attracts more shoppers costs more and spreads a subsidy over more baskets.
   - **Comparison kept:** the MAIN v2 versions, which use \(R_j\), are still computed and saved next to the model versions: `dcs_total_observed_Q` and `cost_spec` in `results/chris_run1_base/lever_curves.csv`.

## Card by card

### Utility

**eq_02 Systematic utility (§1.1.1)** and **eq_22 Step A, pre-subsidy utility (§1.4)**
[eq_02](../figures/equations_v3/eq_02_systematic_utility.png) · [eq_22](../figures/equations_v3/eq_22_step_a_pre_utility.png)

- **MAIN v2:** \(V_{ij} = \beta_{\text{sqft}}\,\text{sqft}_j - \beta_{p,i}\,p_j - \beta_{d,i}\,d_{tj}\)
- **Model as run:** \(V_{ij} = \alpha_j + \gamma_{sq}(sq_j/1000) - \beta_{p,i}\, b_i\,(p_j - \bar p) - \beta_d\, d_{ij}\)
- **What changed:**
  - Store-type constant \(\alpha_j\) added (Change 1).
  - Size measured per 1,000 sq ft.
  - Price per trip and relative to \(\bar p\) (Change 3).
  - One distance coefficient for all income groups.
- **Purpose:**
  - **Size and distance coefficients:** \(\gamma_{sq} = 0.0098\) and \(\beta_d = 0.548\) are borrowed from Hillier et al. (2017, Table 2: main effect plus urban interaction). Their size variable is read as per 1,000 sq ft, the only reading that gives sensible utilities.
  - **One distance coefficient:** Hillier reports one distance effect, so there's no source for an income gradient. A weaker high-income distance effect (−25%) is tested only in sensitivity run 2 (`high_income_distance_scale`).
  - **Price coefficient:** \(\beta_{p,i}\) is derived per income group from the value of travel time, then scaled (Change 4).
- **Code:** `model.utility`.

Step A is the same equation evaluated at baseline prices with the 9 existing stores, so it changed for the same reasons.

### Outside option in probabilities and welfare

**eq_06 Choice probability (§1.1.3)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_06_choice_probability.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(s_{ij} = e^{V_{ij}} / \sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\)
- **Model as run:** \(s_{ij} = e^{V_{ij}} / \left(1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\right)\)
- **What changed:** "1 +" in the denominator.
- **Purpose:** the outside option (Change 2). The probabilities across the modeled stores now sum to less than 1, and the remainder is the share of trips to other stores, about 49% at baseline.
- **Code:** `model.shares`.

**eq_08 Expected maximum utility (§1.1.5)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_08_expected_max_utility.png)
- **Model as run:** \(E[\max_k U_{ik}] = \ln\!\left(1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\right) + \gamma\)
- **Purpose:** the best option now includes other stores, so the logsum includes its \(e^0 = 1\). This keeps welfare consistent with the choice probabilities in eq_06. Both come from the same Gumbel assumption, so they must share the same choice set.
- **Code:** `model.logsum`.

**eq_09 Annual welfare (§1.1.5)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_09_annual_welfare.png)
- **Model as run:** \(W_i = \frac{T}{\beta_{p,i}} \ln\!\left(1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\right)\)
- **Purpose:** the same as eq_08. The code never computes \(W_i\) on its own, only its change in eq_10. The card is updated so the welfare definition matches.

**eq_10 Change in consumer surplus (§1.1.5)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_10_delta_cs.png)
- **Model as run:** \(\Delta CS = \sum_i \frac{n_i T}{\beta_{p,i}} \left\{ \ln\!\left[1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}^{\text{post}}}\right] - \ln\!\left[1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}^{\text{pre}}}\right] \right\}\)
- **Purpose:** the outside option (Change 2). Households who switch from other stores to a subsidized store now count as gaining. This is the main welfare channel for a new store or a deep discount.
- **Code:** `model.delta_cs_i`.

**eq_24 Step C, logsums (§1.4)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_24_step_c_logsum.png)
- **Model as run:** \(L_i = \ln\!\left(1 + \sum_k e^{V_{ik}}\right)\), before and after the policy.
- **Purpose:** the same as eq_10. Step D (eq_25) keeps its MAIN form, \(\sum_i \frac{n_i T}{\beta_{p,i}}(L_i^{\text{post}} - L_i^{\text{pre}})\), so it was not re-rendered.

### Utility after a policy

**eq_15 Post-subsidy utility, v1 (§1.3.1)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_15_post_utility_v1.png),
**eq_17 Post-discount utility, v2 (§1.3.2)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_17_post_utility_v2.png),
**eq_23 Step B (§1.4)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_23_step_b_post_utility.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(V^{\text{post}} = V^{\text{pre}} + \beta_{p,i}\,\Delta p\)
- **Model as run:** \(V^{\text{post}} = V^{\text{pre}} + \beta_{p,i}\, b_i\,\Delta p\)
- **What changed:** baskets per trip \(b_i\) multiplies the price cut.
- **Purpose:** this follows directly from Change 3. Price enters utility as \(\beta_{p,i} b_i p_j\), so a cut of \(\Delta p\) per basket raises utility by \(\beta_{p,i} b_i \Delta p\): the saving on a whole trip, not on one basket.
- **How the code applies it:**
  - The code doesn't add the term explicitly. It lowers the price (`p1[:, j] -= ...` in `lever_cost_cut`, `p1 = p0 * (1 - depth * kappa)` in `lever_contract`) and calls `utility` again. The result is identical.
  - **For N.Y.C. Groceries:** the store isn't in the baseline choice set, so it has no \(V^{\text{pre}}\). Its post-policy utility comes directly from eq_02 at the discounted price. Its \(\Delta CS\) counts only the discount's gain, \(\Delta CS(30\%) - \Delta CS(0\%)\). A plain logit credits any added store with welfare just for existing (a copy of Key Food adds about \$0.9M a year at the unscaled \(\beta_p\)), so that part isn't identified and is reported separately as `entry_gain_not_identified`.

### Volume and pass-through (FRESH tax break and rent subsidy)

**eq_12 Basket volume (§1.2.1)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_12_basket_volume.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(Q_j = R_j / p_j\), using observed revenue.
- **Model as run:** \(\hat Q_j = \hat R_j / p_j\), with \(\hat R_j = \sum_i n_i T s_{ij} b_i p_j\) at baseline.
- **Purpose:** from the `lever_cost_cut` docstring: with the model's own volume, a pass-through of \(\theta = 1\) hands back about \(s\) to shoppers, whatever the store's calibration fit. Calibration only matches sales by store type, so individual stores are over- or under-predicted. With observed \(R_j\), under-predicted stores would look worse for reasons unrelated to the policy.
- **Comparison kept:** the observed-\(Q_j\) result is saved as `dcs_total_observed_Q`.

**eq_14 Price cut, v1 (§1.3.1)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_14_price_cut_v1.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(\Delta p_j = \theta s / Q_j\), \(\theta \in \{0.40, 0.65, 0.85\}\)
- **Model as run:** \(\Delta p_j = \theta s / \hat Q_j\), \(\theta \in \{0.25, 0.50, 0.75\}\), base 0.50.
- **Purpose:**
  - **Volume:** \(\hat Q_j\), as in eq_12.
  - **New \(\theta\) set:** chosen by the team on 2026-10-02 (`params.THETA_FRESH`). No published pass-through rate applies, because a fixed-cost subsidy need not move a profit-maximizing store's prices at all (Weyl and Fabinger 2013). So \(\theta\) is a scenario, and the range was centered on 0.5.
  - **Best case:** run 3 adds \(\theta = 1\).
  - **N.Y.C. Groceries contract:** \(\theta = 1\) (`THETA_CONTRACT`), because Affordability Payments guarantee the discount.
- **Code:** `model.lever_cost_cut`.

### Cost of the 30% contract

**eq_18 Store-level core-basket share (§1.3.2)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_18_store_core_share.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(\kappa_j = \sum_i s_{ij} n_i \kappa_g / \sum_i s_{ij} n_i\)
- **Model as run:** \(\kappa_j = \sum_i n_i s_{ij}^{\text{post}} b_i \kappa_g / \sum_i n_i s_{ij}^{\text{post}} b_i\)
- **What changed:**
  - Weights include baskets per trip \(b_i\).
  - Shares are post-policy.
- **Purpose:**
  - **The calculation:** the code computes \(\kappa_j\) as core-item sales divided by total sales at the store (`core_sales / sales_post` in `lever_contract`). Sales are weighted by baskets bought, so higher-spending households (larger \(b_i\)) weigh more. \(T\) and \(p_j\) cancel out of the ratio.
  - **Why post-policy shares:** the discount is paid on what shoppers buy after it starts, including shoppers the discount attracts.

**eq_19 Gross discount cost (§1.3.2)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_19_gross_discount_cost.png)
- **MAIN v2:** \(0.30\,\kappa_j R_j\)
- **Model as run:** \(0.30\,\kappa_j \hat R_j^{\text{post}}\)
- **Purpose:** Change 5. The discount's cost follows the extra demand it creates. \(\hat R_j^{\text{post}}\) is post-policy predicted sales valued at pre-discount shelf prices, since 30% is given up on the full shelf price of core items.
- **Comparison kept:** the MAIN version, with observed \(R_j\), is reported as `cost_spec`.

**eq_20 Affordability Payment (§1.3.2)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_20_affordability_payment.png) and **eq_21 Total subsidy cost (§1.3.2)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_21_total_subsidy_cost.png)
- **Model as run:**
  - \(\text{AP}_j = \max(0,\; 0.30\,\kappa_j \hat R_j^{\text{post}} - \text{Rent}_j - \text{Tax}_j)\)
  - \(s_j^* = \max(\text{Rent}_j + \text{Tax}_j,\; 0.30\,\kappa_j \hat R_j^{\text{post}})\)
- **Purpose:** these inherit the gross discount cost from eq_19, so they change for the same reason. The contract's structure is unchanged: NYCEDC pays rent and tax, plus whatever the discount costs beyond that.
- **Code:** `cost_model` and `affordability_payment` in `lever_contract`.

### Minimum subsidy

**eq_28 Minimum subsidy for a target gain (§1.5)** [card](../figures/equations_v3/eq_28_min_subsidy.png)
- **MAIN v2 (v1 only):** \(s_j^* = \min_{s \ge 0} s\) subject to \(\Delta CS_j(s) \ge \Delta CS_{\min}\)
- **Model as run (every lever):** \(s_j^* = \min\{ s \in [0, \bar s_j] : \Delta CS_j(s) \ge \Delta CS_{\min} \}\)
- **What changed:**
  - **A cap \(\bar s_j\) on each lever:**
    - the tax the store owes, \(\text{Tax}_j\) (FRESH tax break);
    - its rent, \(\text{Rent}_j\) (rent subsidy);
    - or the cost of the full 30% discount (contract).
  - **Every lever:** the rule applies to all levers, not only v1.
  - **Fixed targets:** \$50K, \$100K, \$250K, \$500K and \$1M (`params.CS_TARGETS`).
- **Purpose:**
  - **Cap:** a tax break can't exceed the tax owed, and a rent subsidy can't exceed the rent. So some targets can't be reached at some stores. These are reported as "not reachable" instead of being solved with an impossible subsidy.
  - **Search method:** the code sweeps each lever from 0% to 100% of its cap on a 21-point grid (`run_model.GRID`), producing a \(\Delta CS(s)\) curve. It then inverts the curve, taking the first grid point that reaches the target and interpolating linearly from the point before.
  - **Contract levers:** the cost never falls below \(\text{Rent}_j + \text{Tax}_j\), because the grid varies the discount depth from 0% to 30%, and at 0% the gain is zero. Effectively, the search starts at rent plus tax rather than at \$0.
- **Code:** `run_model.lever_curves`, `run_model.subsidy_for_targets`.

## Equations not re-rendered

- **Unchanged in form (the MAIN v2 cards still apply):**
  - eq_01 random utility
  - eq_03 Manhattan distance
  - eq_04 consumer groups
  - eq_05 total households
  - eq_11 store revenue (still the calibration target)
  - eq_16 discount price cut \(0.30\,\kappa_g p_j\)
  - eq_25 Step D
  - eq_26 subsidy efficiency (`dcs_per_dollar`)
  - eq_27 post-discount price
- **Equivalent:** eq_13 market size \(M\). The code's total spending, \(\sum_i n_i T b_i \bar p\), equals \(\sum_g N_g \bar f_g \cdot 52\).
- **Not computed by the code:** eq_07 aggregate market share \(S_j\). The code works with sales (\(\hat R_j\)) instead.

**New in the code, with no MAIN v2 equation:** these were left out of the cards by choice.
- Outside option, \(V_{i0} = 0\)
- Baskets per trip \(b_i\)
- \(\beta_p\) derived from the value of travel time, and its capacity scaling
- \(\alpha\) contraction
- Discount-only gain for the new store
- Capital-cost annuity (run 4)
