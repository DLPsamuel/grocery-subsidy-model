Yes, **p_j is one of your 6 R rows**, and with Samuel on rent and square footage it's now your main open task.

## What p_j means

It's the price of one fixed "basket" of groceries (the same items everywhere) at each candidate store. In the model it does two things:

1. **It drives store choice.** Utility includes −β_p · p_j, so cheaper stores win more shoppers.
2. **It converts dollars to baskets.** Q_j = Revenue_j ÷ p_j, and the price drop is Δp = θ · s ÷ Q_j.

**What matters most is how prices differ between stores, not their exact level.** If every store charged the same, price would have no effect on store choice.

## Good news: part of it already exists

I downloaded the Crossa 2019 dataset (NYC DOHMH, on GitHub). It priced the same 10-item basket at 163 NYC supermarkets, **31 of them in the Bronx**. One of them is our store:

- **Key Food, 1050 Westchester Ave (CD2): $21.21** in 2019, with all 10 items found
